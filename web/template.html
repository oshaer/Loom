Loom

Created by Katie Dunbar on November 18, 1996.This is a version from 2026.

Katie Dunbar wrote the first version of Loom in the fall of 1996, as a doctoral student at the MIT AI Lab. Her question was how a group of learning programs should decide whose reports to believe. In Loom, eight agents she called buggles learn by trial and error to cross a maze and post what they find to a shared board. Two of them have faulty sensors, and the others are never told which. Each buggle builds a record of how often its reports agree with what the others see, and the board weighs every report by that record. Katie wanted to know whether a group can learn from all of its members without being led astray by the few who are wrong.

What you will see

S is the start and G the finish; ~ is mud, which costs two extra turns. The numbers are buggles, each in its own color, and * marks two or more in one square.

x is a dead end the team believes, and ? is a dead-end claim it is ignoring, almost always from a faulty buggle. Green dots are the route the team has learned; while the route is still incomplete it shows in teal.

In the panel, watch the Trust column. The Sensor column is there for you: the team is never told which buggles are faulty. When the run ends, the full log appears below, written for readers outside the field.
